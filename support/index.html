Rewarded support

Help with your family’s rewards, goals, and shared devices.

Contact the developer

Have a question or found a problem? Send a support request to the maintainer, MrSeizy, on GitHub.

Open a support request  ↗

A GitHub account is required. Requests are public: use made-up names and omit family photos, balances, private notes, passwords, and Apple Account information. A parent or guardian should submit requests for a child.

Common questions

How do I invite a co-parent or a kid’s device?

On the family owner’s device, open Settings → General → Family Sharing → “Invite a co-parent or kid device.” Choose “Can make changes” for another parent or “View only” for a kid’s device, then send the invitation.

Install Rewarded on the invited device and open the invitation link there. Each participant needs to be signed in to iCloud with their own Apple Account. Once the family arrives, a kid’s device may ask whose device it is. A device can manage or join one family at a time.

My family or latest changes aren’t showing up.
  1. Check that each device is online and signed in to iCloud, with iCloud Drive enabled.
  2. Open Rewarded on each device and allow time for iCloud to sync. Background updates are scheduled by iOS and may arrive later.
  3. Check any iCloud status message in the app. If the family owner’s iCloud storage is full, they need to free space for changes to sync.
  4. For an invited device, make sure it opened the correct invitation. If the invitation was revoked, ask the owner for a new one.

Avoid deleting the app while there may be unsynced changes. If the problem continues, contact support with the exact error text, after removing personal information.

Why can’t my kid add money or edit a goal?

A kid’s device is intentionally read-only. Parents add rewards, approve spending, complete jobs, and manage goals. “Ask a parent” opens a message or share sheet so a kid can choose a parent and send a request; it does not approve the request or change the balance.

Why hasn’t a recurring allowance appeared?

Allowances are recorded by the designated family owner’s device. Check that the schedule is enabled and its start date and weekday are correct, then open Rewarded on that device. iOS controls background refresh, so a scheduled allowance may appear when the app next runs rather than at an exact time.

Rewarded records money or points your family tracks; it does not transfer funds or make bank payments.

How do I manage notifications and widgets?

Parents can manage co-parent alerts and logging reminders in Settings → General → Notifications & reminders. Kid devices can manage the Sunday recap in My style → Notifications. Also check iOS Settings → Notifications → Rewarded.

Widgets display the latest information the device has received. If a widget looks out of date, open Rewarded and let it sync. Background delivery depends on iOS; alerts and widget updates are not guaranteed to arrive immediately.

How do I save a goal from Safari?

On a parent’s device, open Rewarded once so it can recognize the device’s role. In Safari, open a product page, tap Share, and choose “Add to Rewarded.” You may need to enable it in the share sheet’s app list.

Save the suggestion, then return to Rewarded to choose a kid and review the goal. Some pages do not provide a usable price or photo; enter missing details yourself. Check the currency and whether the kid uses money or points before saving.

How do I export or delete family information?

On a parent device, open Settings → General → Export history to create a CSV for one kid or the whole family. Exports include names and notes, so share them only with people you choose.

Delete a kid under Settings → General → Kids to remove their related history, goals, behaviors, praise, jobs, and allowances. The family owner can manage participants through Family Sharing. Deleting the app alone does not remove the family’s iCloud records. See the privacy policy’s data controls for more details.

What to include in a support request

Your device model, iOS or iPadOS version, Rewarded version if available, whether the device belongs to an owner, co-parent, or kid, and the steps that led to the problem. Describe what you expected and what happened instead. Use generic examples and remove personal details from error messages.

The developer cannot see your private family database or recover deleted iCloud records. Never send a password, verification code, or exported family history.
